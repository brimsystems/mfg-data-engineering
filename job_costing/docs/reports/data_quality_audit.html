Section 1

Job Costing ERP Implementation

Within the shop's ERP system, new job costing functionality was added to track the estimated and actual cost of every job. This report details the changes made to the ERP system to capture and monitor job cost detail, as well as the data quality audit that improved the accuracy of these job cost figures. These changes enabled the findings in the Job Margin Analytics Diagnostic and the monitoring capabilities shown in the Job Cost Dashboard.

As a result of this implementation, we made numerous changes to the shop's data sources to improve the accuracy of the ERP's estimated and actual job cost figures, as summarized below.

Changes to improve estimated job costs

  • When a quote line becomes a job, its part-level estimate is now written onto the Jobs table by cost element, at the ordered quantity.
  • Repeat parts, which ran on standing prices from previous quotes with no current estimate, now receive a current-cost estimate computed monthly from today's material prices, work-center rates and measured cycle times.
  • Routing standards for repeat parts were replaced with cycle and setup times measured from machine data.
  • Own products, previously priced from a launch-date standard plus annual adjustment, now carry a standard cost refreshed with the repeat parts.
  • The single blended shop rate was replaced with rate pools by work center, so both estimated and actual costs now reflect actual machine and labor costs.
  • Vendor minimum charges and current prices now live in the quoting module.

Changes to improve actual job costs

  • Machine monitoring on the CNC cells was connected to the ERP, and actual machine hours now serve as the basis for run time on those cells.
  • Setup, run, rework and indirect time are now recorded separately, improving each job's labor cost accuracy.
  • Purchase orders for outside processing now require a job number when created, so actual vendor costs connect to each job.
  • Labor is now captured at every operation, including the secondary cells that previously posted none, and clock records no longer run across breaks and shifts or cover several machines at once.

Section 2

Data Quality Audit

Section 2.1

Findings

Over the 36 months from July 2023 to June 2026, 389K records were produced across the ERP's 10 tables. This audit reviewed all of them and found 17 types of data quality error recurring over the period: seven at the master-level tables and 10 at the transaction-level tables. An additional 1.6 million rows of machine monitoring data were reviewed, but no data quality issues were found. As a result of these data errors across the ERP system, both the estimated and actual job cost figures were inaccurate and unable to be relied upon.

Known Limitations

  • Machine hours split across a part's open jobs. Before the monitoring feed carried job numbers, machine time is assigned by program to part and then to the jobs open for it; where several jobs of one part were open at once the interval is split by job quantity and flagged. 1,328 of the 12,064 jobs with machine hours carry a split. The part's hours are right in total, but a long job that overlapped another of the same part can carry too many or too few of them, and the margin diagnostic notes the one customer where this moves a reported figure.
  • Purchase orders attributed to a job of a different size. Of the lines re-tied to jobs by vendor, service, quantity and receipt window, 32 had a quantity outside half to twice the job's quantity. They are treated as not attributed: they stay in the ledger residual and are allocated with it.
Section 2.4

Process Changes

The error remediation in Section 2.2 corrected the ERP's records. The changes in this section are designed to keep them cleaned, and fall into two categories.

The first category is the ERP system settings. These are configured once, take effect for every user, and stop most of the errors at the point of entry. This category holds on its own. The table lists each change, what it does and the errors it closes (the error can no longer occur) or addresses (the error is caught or reduced, but not prevented).

Appendix B

Job Costing Process, as Issued to the Shop

One page, for whoever runs job cost next. In force since engagement week 6 (05/11/2026). Owner: Controller.

1. What every job goes through

A job exists to be compared with its estimate. Every transaction below carries the job number, and the reporting layer builds job cost from the transactions, never from a manual entry.

Where the estimate comes from. A quote is priced per part number at several quantity breaks (for example 25, 50, 100 and 250 pieces). New quoted work takes the quote line's estimate at the ordered quantity: the ERP picks the break nearest the ordered quantity and applies its figures per piece, so a quantity between breaks carries a small, known error from setup amortized at the break's quantity. Repeat parts release against a standing price, not a new quote, and take the current-cost estimate the pipeline computes monthly for every repeat part: today's material prices, the work-center rate pools and the measured cycle and setup times on the routing, at the released quantity. Own products take the standard cost on the part, which the controller reviews each quarter against current cost. In every case the quoting module prices outside processing at the vendor's current price and no less than the vendor's minimum charge.

2. When a scan or a record is missing

Nothing is filled in by hand. If an operation has no scan, no terminal record and no machine hours by the time the next operation starts, the reporting layer costs it at the routing standard and tags the element estimated. The tag stays on the job; it is not cleared by a later correction unless the transaction is found and posted. A clock record that cannot be trusted (left open across a shift with no machine data behind it, or charged to a job whose routing does not fit) is tagged unrepairable and its hours are shown but not relied on.

The cell lead sees the missing scans for the cell each morning on the coverage screen and chases them that day. A missing scan found within the week is posted with its true times; after that it stays estimated.

3. How coverage is reported

Coverage is the share of a job's cost that rests on a transaction rather than on a standard. It is reported for every job on its cost screen, and weekly by work center for the production manager. On completed jobs released since the process went live it stands at 99% measured, 1% estimated; scan coverage at the secondary operations stood at 90.5% in week 12. The remainder is named on each job: which operation, which element, and why.

Coverage below 85% on a job holds the job out of the margin reports until the cell lead has reviewed it. Coverage by work center is reviewed weekly; a cell below 85% for two weeks running is raised with the production manager.

4. The monthly repricing review

On the first Tuesday of the month the controller opens the repricing queue: every repeat part against its current cost at today's material prices, the work-center pool rates and the measured standards, with the gap to target on annual volume and what moved since the part was last quoted. The controller and the owner take the parts below cost plus target in order of the annual gap and decide each one: reprice to current cost plus the target markup (the customer is notified with the cost basis), hold with a reason and a date, or exit at the next release. The decision, the new price and the rationale are recorded on the queue. A held part comes back the following month until it is repriced or exited.

The standing price on a repeat part changes only through this review. The annual across-the-board letter is retired.
